# Choose the direction

Select the branch that serves the message and available assets. These profiles
describe decisions to make; they do not mandate a palette, font or shot count.

| Direction | Production decisions | Proof to inspect | Counterexample |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Typography announcement | Type scale, weight, wrapping, alignment and reading windows | A phrase arrives, becomes readable, then yields to the next message | One light-weight centered phrase may be complete; extra labels would compete |
| Product demonstration | Real UI states, action targets, result and camera crop | A visible action causes a visible result in the same product example | A heading beside a generic browser does not prove the feature works |
| Graphic brand film | Brand geometry, palette relationships and diagram logic | One shape or relationship develops into the next statement | Reusing a faint logo in every background does not establish a narrative |
| Dimensional scene | Geometry, material, lighting, shadows and asset/render feasibility | The most revealing angle or material transition | A CSS tilt suits a flat panel but cannot supply every object's physical depth |
| Footage edit | Actual clips, subjects, shot relationships, captions and audio | The sequence of source shots with the intended claim | A component library cannot substitute for missing photographic material |

References can share a device without sharing a direction. Select one primary
reference and borrow a secondary device for a named purpose. Record where a
reference depends on production resources the project does not yet have.

Apply authentic assets and known typography first; choose density for the film.
A moodboard helps unresolved imagery or art direction. It is not a prerequisite
for a wordmark or a supplied visual identity.
